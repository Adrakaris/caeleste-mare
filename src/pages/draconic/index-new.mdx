## Introduction

<Drop>D</Drop>raconic is the language of the dragons in the Material Plane, originating around the Spring of the Earth. It comes in two main dialects: eastern and western, *rékharhákr* and *rkaarik*, with limited intelligibility between them. There is also the ancient literary form of the language “*hakr*”, which is only preserved for highly formal and religious occasions, and is generally not known by outsiders.

This language is explained using the eastern version of Draconic, which is spoken in East Aseron and the Spring of the Earth. Later sections show differences between this, the western dialect, and ancient draconic.

*This is the fourth major iteration of draconic which contains breaking changes with previous versions and previous documents.*

### Introduction

*rekharhakr*, or eastern draconic, is the variant of draconic spoken around and east of the Spring of the Earth, in East Aseron and the Sea of Shattered Stars. 

It is the native language of dragonkind, though often learned as a second language by merchants and scholars, and used as somewhat of a lingua-franca across the east. _rekharhakr_ is considered "colloquial", used for casual literature or messages, but not in governance or religious ceremony. The *khankain* ("civilised" dragonkind) of the central plains prefer to use the central plains Yan language [read: Classical Chinese] for state affairs, or Ancient Draconic for solemn and religious matters.

***Long vowels*** are written using reduplicated vowel letters *aa, ee, ii, oo, uu*, however of these only *aa, ii* are common. This elongates the vowel sound but does not change its quality significantly. Vowel diphthongs include *ai* /aɪ/ and *ie* /ie/,

In ancient times the merged sounds *h* and *kh*, *s* and *th* used to have different qualities, though this is now reduced to a tone distinction.

#### 1.2. Pitch Accent

Pitch accent occurs from sound mergers in the language, and are applied per syllable. 

*th* and *kh* are low-tones, syllables with these should be pronounced at the low end of the vocal range.

*s* and *h* are high-tones, syllables with these should be pronounced at normal, or slightly higher than normal vocal range.

***Exception.*** A word-final *s* does not affect the tone of that syllable, as it is usually silent.

#### 1.3. Syllables and Stress

*rekharhakr* syllables are flexible, and usually have a **(C)(C)V(C)(C)** structure. Difficult consonant pairs (e.g. _rk_ and _dn_) are permitted (due to descent from ancient draconic), but usually avoided.

Stress is highly irregular and word-dependent. In romanisation, an áccent over a vowel indicates the stressed syllable. Stress _slightly_ prefers second syllable stress, ignoring most grammatical suffixes. Verb conjugation suffixes can shift stress.

Stress in general can be accent-dependent, and in most cases stress is not an important factor in word legibility.

Long vowels (*aa*, *ii*, etc., or dipthongues *ai*, *ae*) are preferred for stress instead of short vowels. Where this is an exception (e.g. *ásiir* for "sun" vs *asiir* for "Asyr") an áccent is used.

### 2. Orthography 

#### 2.1. Alphabet

The draconic alphabet is called the *akron*, after its first five letters. Each letter is given a name of a word beginning with it. Traditionally, only majuscule script existed, though minuscule "scribe-hand" developed in Dacrame in the west, leading to western draconic having minuscule letters.

This alphabet originated in West Aseron, west of the mountains, and spread eastwards. In the east, it is seen as an informal script which is not held in high regard by dragonkind, merely being seen as useful for communication. Often, the draconic alphabet is used to annotate Yan or other languages' texts as a pronunciation gloss.

#### 2.2. Spelling

Draconic spelling is largely regular--each letter is pronounced. 

The only exception is a final-s, which is dropped to some extent in most accents of all modern dialects.

Romanisation and spelling correspond exactly, with the digraphs *th* and *kh* representing single letters.

> [!comment]
> *othalon* / O Th A L O N.  \[<Drac>OTHALON</Drac>\]    
> *vathas* / V A Th A S.  \[<Drac>VATHAS</Drac>\]

**s-*dropping.*** Final-s sounds are usually silent, being turned into a glottal stop or lengthening the previous syllable slightly.

> [!comment]
> *váthas* / depths.     
> `DEPTHS`. /vasas/ or /vasa/ or /vasa:/

### 3. Grammar Overview

Below is a quick overview of grammar in _rekharhakr_.

***Word order.*** SVO word order + topic comment structure. SOV accepted sometimes in literature.

***Morphology.*** _rekharhakr_ is a mostly **isolating** language with some agglutinative features.

***Marking.*** Dependent-marking: grammatical relationships (e.g. possessives) are marked on the dependent (e.g. the thing doing the posessing)

***Subject alignment.*** Nominative-accusative like English or Chinese.

***Head-initial vs head-final.*** Mostly head-initial like in English or Chinese; **head-final** for adjectives, adverbs, and other descriptors (e.g. titles).

***Dropping.*** _rekharhakr_ is not a pro-drop language, subjects and objects are usually required. Due to influences from the central plains, dropping is acceptable but not mainstream.

***Honorifics.*** _rekharhakr_ has a very basic honorific system that splits the second person pronoun. 

### 4. Noun Morphology

#### 4.1. Nouns

Nouns are simple words. _rekharhakr_ does not possess noun gender/class, noun agreeement, or mark plurality. 

Articles do not exist, and number / count is indicated simply by putting the number word before the noun.

> [!comment]
> _yir rekhar, zor rekhar_ / one dragon, five dragons.     
> `ONE DRAGON, FIVE DRAGON`

Definite emphasis is done with _hir_ "this" or _tai_ "that".





#### 4.2. Pronouns

Pronouns are differentiated by person, gender, and for 1st and 2nd person pronouns by honorific. Pronouns are not differentiated by case.

Person | Pronoun type | Word(s)
-|-|-
1st | singular normal | _zar, za_
·   | singular royal (royal we) | _valkhán_
·   | singular noble | _várkhan_
·   | singular possessive | _záron, ron_
·   | plural (exclusive) | _zárad_
·   | plural (inclusive) | _ánad_
2nd | singular informal | _zii_
·   | singular respectful | _záhar_
3rd | male | _aak_
·   | female singular | _khaa_
·   | female plural | _khaakad_
·   | ungendered singular "they" | _aa_
·   | ungendered plural "they" | _saa_
·   | lower "it" | _thaa_
·   | lower plural "they" | _thiaa_

_-ad_ is the standard suffix for pronoun plurality and is exclusive by default. _ánad_ is used for the inclusive we. This becomes _-kad_ when the pronoun ends with _a_ (e.g. _khaakad_). Third person ungendered pronouns have a different word.

"lower" usually refers to non-dragons, and can apply to both non-sapient things and sapient creatures (e.g. humans, elves, dwarves, etc.). Anything that is of higher "standing" than a dragon (or viewed with sufficient respect) is referred to by standard third person pronouns. 

#### 4.3. Reflexives

Reflexives ("yourself", "myself") are constructed using the word _ilím_ "self" after the noun, e.g. _za ilim_ "myself".

The second person informal reflexive is shortened to _ziim_ "yourself"

#### 4.4. Fused Relatives

The "he, who" or "she, who" construction, or the pronoun "whoever" is a fused relative. In _rekharhakr_, this is expressed by repeating the pronoun.

> [!comment]
> _aak, aak anavín yira_ / He who lights the fire     
> `HE, HE LIGHT FIRE`

#### 4.5. Possession

Possession is marked by the suffix *-on* on the possesser.

> [!comment]
> _rekharon oraes_ / Dragon's zither.     
> `DRAGON-poss ZITHER`

For the first person singular _zar_, the possessive is informally shortened from _záron_ to _ron_.

> [!comment]
> _ron ixiir_ / My love.     
> `I-poss BELOVED`

Plural endings on pronouns always come before possession.

#### 4.6. Determiners

_rekharhakr_ has a two level determinative system like English, _hir_ "this" vs _tak_ "that"; _tan_ "here" vs _tak_ "there".

### 5. Verb Morphology

There are regular and irregular verbs in _rekharhakr_. 

Regular verbs mostly end with _-in_ and conjugate consistently. Rarely, a regular verb will end with a different vowel and _-n_.

Irregular verbs end with _-n_ and conjugate inconsistently.

#### 5.1. Tense

Verbs conjugate by tense.

Tense | Suffix | Example/notes
-|-|-
Infinitive/present | _-in_ | _aak paziin_ / he plays<br/>Present continuous is conjugated separately
Past perfect/imperfect | _ins_ | _aak paziins_ / he played<br/>Add _s_
Past pluperfect | _inir_ | _aak paziinir / he had played<br/>Add _ir_
Future | _ix_ | _aak paziix_ / he will play<br/>Replace _-n_ with _-x_
Conditional | _ixa_ | _aak paziixa_ / he would play<br/>Replace _-n_ with _-xa_; also used for talking about the future in past tense
Imperative | _ii_ / - | _pazii nal za_ or _paz nal za_ (informal) / play with me<br/>Replace _in_ or _iin_ with _ii_; informally remove _in_ or replace _iin_ with _ii_

***To be.*** _siin_ (to be) is an example of an irregular verb. Below is the table of its conjugation.

Tense | Form
-|-
Infinitive | _siin_
Present | _siin_
Past | _is_
Pluperfect | _is_
Future | _xii_
Conditional | _xia_
Imperative | _sii_
_Copula_ | _sn_

Irregular verb conjugations are noted in compact form in the dictionary.

***Continuous form.*** Continuous form (e.g. going, playing) is indicated by having the verb _siin_ modally before the main verb. _siin_ and the main verb have to agree tenses.

> [!comment]
> _za siin paziin_ / I am playing    
> _za is paziins_ / I was playing    
> _za xii paziix_ / I will be playing

#### 5.2. Aspect and Modal Particles

Modal particles, such as _kat_ (can), _iir_ (may), _os_ (must), _has_ (shall), _sis_ (want) affect the aspect of a verb. These **don't** conjugate, and go in front of a verb. They are recorded under "MVb" in the dictionary.

> [!comment]
> _za kat paziin_ / I can play     
> `I CAN PLAY`

#### 5.3. Negation

Negation is expressed by the modal particle _da_. This goes before the verb or modal aspect. 

_da_ in isolation is only used for emphasis. Normally, the **prefix** _d-_ is used instead. This is appended directly onto the verb or modal particle, and changes form based on the continent afterwards:

| original         | transformed | original | transformed |
| ---------------- | ----------- | -------- | ----------- |
| *da*             | *da*        | *dx*     | *dix*       |
| *dk*             | *dkh*       | *dm*     | *dv*        |
| *dn*             | *dan*       | *dl*     | *dr*        |
| *dd*             | *vd*        | *dp*     | *dv*        |
| *dt*             | *dz*        | *dg*     | *dig*       |


> [!comment]
> *za danadéns thaa* / I did not see it
> `I not-SEE-past IT` (note ***dn**adéns* becomes ***dan**adéns*)

>[!comment]
> *za dkhat paziin* / I can't play     
> `I not-CAN PLAY` (note ***dk**at* becomes ***dkh**at*)


#### 5.4. Acting

To refer to the actor of a verb (the "doer"), the infinitive form is marked by the suffix *-ak*.

> [!comment]
> _karínak sa xarái_ / The dancer in the night      
> `DANCE-actor AT NIGHT`

#### 5.5. Passive Voice

Passive voice is constructed using topic-comment structure with copula.

> [!comment]
> _aa triin thúrgar_ / they eat fish     
> `THEY EAT FISH` (active voice)

> [!comment]
> _thúrgar triin sn_ / the fish is eaten      
> `FISH EAT BE-copula` (passive voice)
